About

I work in the Peng Lab at MIT and the Broad Institute on lanthanide-doped upconverting nanoparticles, which absorb near-infrared light and emit visible light. My focus is making them reproducibly, with narrow size distributions, controlled composition, and surface functionalization strategies that keep them stable in water and inside cells.

Materials characterization is the other half of my work. I regularly use transmission and scanning electron microscopes and perform single-molecule imaging on widefield and confocal microscopes. To aid in data analysis, I wrote psyfit, an open-source app that turns micrographs into per-particle size and shape statistics. Before MIT, I conducted research in physical organic chemistry and soft-matter physics at Williams College.

Things I’ve Built

psyfit

Python, Streamlit

An open-source web app that turns microscope images into measurements. It includes tools for:

  • Electron microscopy. Opens raw TEM files, reads the scale from the instrument metadata, finds each particle, and reports size and shape distributions.
  • Fluorescence microscopy. Locates individual particles in widefield and confocal images and measures how bright each one is.
  • Batch export. Processes whole sets of images at once and writes the results to spreadsheets.
